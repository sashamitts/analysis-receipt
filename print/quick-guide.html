Analysis receipt

Every AI analysis makes compromises. Document yours.

A one-page record that travels with any qualitative analysis, done by people, by AI, or both. It shows what the analysis read, what it set aside, whether anyone checked, and what each finding rests on. It does not certify the work. It lets others judge it.

Four parts, each with an owner

1
QuestionWhat was asked, and what decision it serves.
Whoever set the question
2
ReadersWhat was read, by whom or by what, and what was never read.
Whoever ran the reading
3
LeftoversWhat was set aside, and whether anyone checked it.
Whoever checks the leftovers
4
ClaimsWhat each finding rests on, and the cases against it.
Whoever reports the findings

Five steps

  1. Make a copy before you start. Write the research question in, word for word, before any reading begins.
  2. Fill it in while you work. A record made afterwards is a memory. Note each reader as you use it, including any AI tool, its version and the date. Write counts and reasons, not adjectives. Blank reads as "not checked".
  3. Check the leftovers. The one step that adds new evidence. See the box.
  4. Look for the rare views. A random sample rarely finds what one or two people said. Ask a second reader, a person or an AI, to find the people the question does not fit. Or read the passages your codes fit worst.
  5. Attach it to what you share. Put a short version on the last page or slide, and link the full receipt. Each owner signs their part.

Checking the leftovers

A leftover is a passage no finding used, such as an interview answer no theme quotes.
  1. List the passages no finding used.
  2. Pick some at random.
  3. Read each one. Does it fit an existing finding, hold a new idea, or hold nothing relevant?
  4. Add any new idea to the findings, and say so on the receipt.
Read, nothing newMay still hold a new idea
20 leftoversup to about 14%
30 leftoversup to about 10%
60 leftoversup to about 5%
100 leftoversup to about 3%

At 95% confidence. Pick the size the decision can live with.

Words used here

Reader
Anything that turned material into findings. A person, an AI model, a tool, or a pipeline step.
Passage
The smallest unit that carries one idea, such as one interview answer or one survey response.

Remember. A receipt is not a certificate. A random sample finds common misses, not rare ones. No reading is ground truth, including yours. Agreement between readers is not coverage. The leftover check and the claim checks are what keep it from becoming a ritual.
