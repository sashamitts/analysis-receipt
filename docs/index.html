Analysis receipt

Every AI analysis makes compromises. Document yours.

A one-page record that travels with any qualitative analysis, done by people, by AI, or both. It shows what the analysis read, what it set aside, whether anyone checked, and what each finding rests on.

Make your own copy (Google Doc) Download the printable PDF See a filled example

Free to use and adapt. No sign-up and no code needed. Takes about 15 minutes to set up for a study.

Why a receipt

What an analysis leaves out is invisible in the analysis.

AI output looks complete

AI tools now summarise interviews, cluster survey responses and draft findings in minutes. The output often checks out line by line.

The gaps do not show

A tool may read a sample, cut long files, or drop what only a few people said. Nothing in the output tells you this happened.

The receipt makes them visible

It does not certify the work. It records what the analysis rests on, and what it did not look at, so others can judge it.

People leave things out too. The receipt works for any analysis, with or without AI.

The four parts

Four parts, each with someone who answers for it.

Keep these four, even if you rename the lines under them. One person can own all four. In a team, each owner signs their part.

1

Question

What was asked, and what decision it serves.

Owner: whoever set the question
2

Readers

What was read, by whom or by what, and what was never read.

Owner: whoever ran the reading
3

Leftovers

What was set aside, and whether anyone checked it.

Owner: whoever checks the leftovers
4

Claims

What each finding rests on, and the cases against it.

Owner: whoever reports the findings

How to use it

Five steps.

  1. Make a copy before you start

    Use the Google Doc template or print the PDF. Keep it next to your analysis files. Write your research question in, word for word, before any reading starts.

  2. Fill it in while you work

    A record made afterwards is a memory, and memories leave things out too. Note each reader as you use it, including any AI tool, its version and the date. Write counts, names and reasons, not adjectives. A blank line reads as "not checked". If a line does not apply, write "Not applicable" and one reason.

  3. Check the leftovers

    This is the one step that adds new evidence. A leftover is a passage that no finding used, such as an interview answer that no theme quotes.

    How to do it
    1. List the passages that no finding used.
    2. Pick some at random. Shuffle them, or use a random number generator.
    3. Read each one and sort it. It fits an existing finding, it holds a new idea, or it holds nothing relevant.
    4. If you find a new idea, add it to the findings and say so on the receipt. That is the check working.

    If your sample holds nothing new, it tells you roughly how much could still be missing.

    Leftovers you read, with nothing newShare that may still hold a new idea
    20up to about 14%
    30up to about 10%
    60up to about 5%
    100up to about 3%

    At 95% confidence. Pick the size you can live with for the decision at hand. A design exploration may accept 15%. A policy recommendation may need 5% or less.

  4. Look for the rare views

    A random sample tells you about common misses. It rarely finds what only one or two people said. If rare views matter for the decision, run at least one targeted search. For example, ask a second reader, a person or an AI, to find the people your question does not fit. Or rank the leftovers by how poorly your codes fit them and read the top of the list.

  5. Attach it to what you share

    Put a short version on the last page of the report or the last slide of the readout, and link the full receipt. Each owner signs their part.

Writing good entries

Counts and reasons, not adjectives.

These examples are invented to show the difference.

What was read, and by which readers
Weak
"Analysed with AI."
Strong
"The researcher read all 24 transcripts. The repository's summary feature was given all 24 on 3 March. It does not show what it read, and it cuts transcripts over its length limit. 4 of the 24 were over it."
What was sampled, and what it found
Weak
"Spot-checked the transcripts."
Strong
"60 of 507 leftovers, chosen at random and read by the researcher. 9 fit existing findings, 0 held a new idea, 51 held nothing relevant. Up to about 5% may still hold a new idea."
What each claim rests on
Weak
"Most users want X."
Strong
"Admins avoid bulk import after one bad experience. 9 of 24 participants, counted from coded answers. 2 used it again after a failure. Both had a colleague who restored their data. Reported as a condition."

The receipt from our demonstration.

Twelve interviews from a public dataset, read by me and by four AI setups, for the talk "Finding What AI Analysis Misses". It is small on purpose, so read it as an example of the method, not as a finding.

  • The same wording for every reader

  • Anything beyond handing over, keeping or checking
  • No decision rests on it
Readers
  • 12 interviews drawn at random from 1,000 (138 answers)

  • The other 988 interviews

  • 25 leftovers unread
Claims
  • One AI pass found 56% of the 34 ideas in my reading. All AI readers together found 79%.
  • All 7 ideas no AI found were held by four people or fewer
  • All 526 AI quotes were word for word
  • 3 ideas only the AI proposed. 2 held partly, 1 was already in my reading.
  • Claude matched AI findings to my ideas. I checked 20 at random, and all held.

Limits

What the receipt can and cannot do.

It is not a certificate.A filled receipt does not mean the analysis is good. It means someone can see what it rests on. Weak work with an honest receipt is easier to judge than strong-looking work without one.
A random sample finds common misses, not rare ones.An idea one or two people voiced will usually fall outside a random sample. That is what the targeted search is for.
No reading is ground truth.Any comparison needs a reference reading, and that reader misses things too. Check disagreements against the records before counting them either way.
Agreement is not coverage.Two readers can agree closely and still miss the same things. Agreement only measures what was put in front of them.
Write it during the work.Accounts written afterwards leave things out, whether a person or an AI writes them.
Checklists can become ritual.The leftover check and the claim checks keep the receipt honest, because each one adds new evidence. Without them the rest is description.

Words used here

A short glossary.

Reader
Anything that turned material into findings. A person coding, an AI model summarising, a tool, or a step that decides what a model sees.
Passage
The smallest unit that can carry one idea. Usually one interview answer, one survey response, one observation or one field note.
Leftover
A passage that no finding used. For example, an interview answer that no theme quotes.
Targeted search
A second look aimed at what a random sample misses, such as people the question does not fit, or passages the codes fit badly.
Upper bound
The most that could still be missing, given what your sample found. "Up to 5%" means the true share is probably lower.
